Innovation as a dynamic component of how we audit

Middle market companies operate in a fast-paced business environment where change is constant, information is currency and collaboration is critical. Staying ahead of it all requires innovative technology tailored and implemented through the power of critical thinking. RSM’s expanding ecosystem of technology relationships and advanced digital tools enables our auditors to deliver audit solutions and insights that provide broader business value to your organization.

At RSM, we view innovation as a dynamic component of how we empower our audit professionals to bring more efficiency and value to the audits we perform. Rather than applying a static technology platform across all businesses, as some of our competitors do, the benefit of our audit innovation technology ecosystem is our ability to apply technologies that are tailored to your particular circumstances and to deliver insights that are relevant to your business.

Our innovative approach is supported by three pillars — automation, data analytics and the digital audit experience — to increase efficiency, minimize disruption and surface new information. RSM clients benefit from the digital velocity we employ.

Our technically astute and digitally savvy auditors understand that every business is unique. We consider the issues that are important to you and the complexities of your environment to scale digital tools to meet you where you are and help take you where you want to go. We analyze broad data sets to understand key issues central to your business and industry and bring them into focus through data visualization technologies. Our auditors apply their industry knowledge and understanding of your business to translate information derived from the audit into actionable insights for your business.

Pitch mode — what changed and why

The problem with the page today

The live page is four paragraphs of prose and a video. It has no call to action anywhere — a visitor who finishes reading has nothing to do and nothing to give us. It is a brochure page sitting in an insights funnel.

Every word of that prose is kept here. What is added is a reason to act and a place to act.

What was added

  • Two CTAs in the banner — one to the diagnostic, one to the film. The banner is the only thing every visitor sees.
  • A sticky sub-nav with a persistent contact button, so the ask never scrolls away on a long read.
  • The three pillars become three cards. They were named in one sentence and then never shown; now they are the page's spine.
  • A pull-quote lifts the pillars sentence out of the prose block so the structure is visible before you read it.
  • A one-question diagnostic — the engagement journey below.
  • A sticky bar that appears after the visitor passes the fold and has not converted.

The engagement journey

Three stages, each asking for slightly more than the last — progressive profiling rather than one wall-sized form.

  • Stage 1 — no form. One question: where does your audit lose time? Three options, each mapping to one pillar. The visitor gets a tailored read for free.
  • Stage 2 — two fields. Email and company unlock a two-page brief on the pillar they picked. They have already self-segmented, so the brief is relevant and the ask is small.
  • Stage 3 — the rest. Name, title, revenue and audit timing, in exchange for a walkthrough. Only asked of people who have already raised their hand twice.

Why stage 1 is not gated: gating the diagnostic kills completion. Gating the explanation does not — by the time the read appears the visitor is invested and now has a specific question only the brief answers. Same principle as the Exit Readiness assessment.

Why the answer is the lead signal

The pillar someone picks is a routing instruction. Automation points at process and close-cycle work; analytics points at data and reporting depth; the digital experience points at multi-entity and coordination complexity — which usually means a larger, more complex account.

Every event carries friction, so completions attribute to a pillar instead of one undifferentiated pool.

Tracking

Events push to window.dataLayer for GA4 — open the console to watch them fire.

  • page_cta_click — every CTA, tagged by position
  • diagnostic_select — which friction was picked
  • generate_lead — stage 2 submit, with the pillar attached
  • request_demo — stage 3 submit
  • video_open, journey_abandon

Brand fidelity

  • Tokens read from rsmus.com's own AEM stylesheet — green #3f9c35, headline band #009cd9, navy #27455c, the grey ramp.
  • border-radius: 0 everywhere; headings at weight 300; buttons at 18px/400.
  • The signature move is kept: headline in a solid blue band over a dark hero.
  • High Contrast toggle instead of a dark theme, matching rsmus.com.
  • Photography is referenced from RSM's own DAM, so this uses the real library rather than lookalike stock.
  • Prelo is licensed and self-hosted, so Barlow substitutes at the same weights. Swap --font-sans and it is on-brand for real.

AEM mapping

Class names follow the Core Components convention already in use on rsmus.com — cmp-globalheader, cmp-hero, cmp-button, cmp-cta, cmp-footer — so this reads as a component composition, not a one-off page.

  • Hero, CTA band, resource cards and footer map to existing components
  • The diagnostic is the one new component: a multifield of options, each with a key, a heading and a body
  • Pillar copy, readout copy and form fields are all authorable
  • No server dependency — the page can be cached at the edge

Before this ships

  • Forms have no endpoint — needs marketing automation plus consent and privacy handling
  • Pillar and readout copy is inferred and tagged yellow; practice teams supply the real wording
  • The video is a poster image only — needs the real Brightcove embed
  • Resource cards are a sample selection, not a curated set
  • Copy needs compliance and risk-management review
